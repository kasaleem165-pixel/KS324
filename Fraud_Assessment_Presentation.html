Situation

The Internal Audit team at Beaconhouse Group Regional Office South (Karachi 400) detected anomalies in the facial recognition attendance system (BEAMS). The Manager IT — the custodian of the attendance system — is alleged to have exploited administrative access rights to back-date and alter his own "Time In" entries to conceal persistent late arrivals or absences.

Red Flags Identified

  • Missing "Time In" entries replaced post-facto with back-dated records
  • "Time In" recorded at exact departure ("Time Out") time on same day
  • Original "Time In" overwritten with an earlier, fabricated timestamp
  • 96 entries clustered in the 1-minute window 08:59:01–09:00:59 over 17 months
  • Subject absent from CCTV footage during the times his system record claims presence
  • Attendance for two separate days processed simultaneously on a later date
  • No supporting documentation or authorization for modifications

Fraud Hypothesis

Mr. Syed Khurram Hussain (Manager IT, EID 33902) systematically exploited exclusive administrative access to the BEAMS attendance system to fabricate, back-date, and overwrite his "Time In" records. The purpose was to misrepresent habitual late arrivals and/or absences as timely reporting, thereby obtaining salary, benefits, or avoiding disciplinary consequences he would not otherwise be entitled to — constituting occupational fraud (payroll / time-and-attendance fraud).

Why It Matters

  • Undermines integrity of the organization's attendance control environment
  • Creates financial loss through unearned pay / benefits
  • Represents abuse of a privileged IT role and position of trust
  • Pattern over 17+ months suggests willful, not accidental, conduct
  • Systemic risk: same privileges could be used to manipulate others' records

Scheme Mechanics

Method A — Retroactive "Time In" Creation

No "Time In" was recorded during morning hours. At end of day, when marking "Time Out", the subject simultaneously punched a back-dated "Time In" value set at ≈08:59–09:00, making it appear he arrived on time. System accepted the back-dated entry due to admin privileges.

8 confirmed instances — Oct 2025

Method B — Overwriting an Existing Late "Time In"

A genuine late "Time In" was recorded (e.g., 09:34 or 10:20). The subject subsequently replaced it with an earlier, fabricated timestamp (e.g., 08:50 or 08:59) using admin access, erasing evidence of the original late arrival.

2 confirmed instances — 3 Oct & 24 Oct 2025

Method C — Cross-Day Back-Dating

Attendance for one working day was not recorded on that day at all. On the following day, both the previous day's fabricated "Time In" and the current day's entry were submitted together, creating the appearance of two consecutive on-time arrivals.

1 confirmed instance — 3 & 4 Nov 2025

Pattern Analysis — Historical Data Significance

Annexure H documents 96 entries between 08:59:01 and 09:00:59 over the period July 2024 to November 2025. Statistical significance: natural attendance distributions do not cluster within a 2-minute window with this frequency. The probability of 96 entries falling within a 120-second window purely by chance, across 17 months of daily arrivals, is astronomically low — consistent only with deliberate, systematic back-dating targeting the exact reporting threshold. This constitutes strong circumstantial evidence of a long-running, intentional scheme.
