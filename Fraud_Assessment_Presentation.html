Situation

C&R Division detected anomalies in the ATLAS facial recognition attendance system. The Systems Administrator — system custodian — allegedly exploited admin access to back-date and alter his "Time In" entries, concealing late arrivals and absences.

Red Flags

  • RF-1 | SOMOTO: Auditor observed routine late arrivals and late departures, yet no corresponding leave deductions were recorded.
  • RF-2 | Pattern: 96 "Time In" entries over 17 months all fall within seconds of the 09:00 threshold — statistically impossible by chance.
⚠ Hypothesis A — Guilty

Mr. Shakeel knowingly misused admin access to fabricate "Time In" records, concealing late arrivals to avoid salary deductions and disciplinary action — constituting payroll / time-and-attendance fraud.

✓ Hypothesis B — Not Guilty
  • System glitch caused missed morning scans; corrections made in good faith without knowing supervisor approval was needed.
  • Subject believed late sittings entitled him to offset late arrivals informally, unaware of the formal policy and approval process.

Scheme Mechanics

Method A — Retroactive Creation

No morning scan. At departure, subject punched a back-dated "Time In" of ≈08:59 via admin access.

8 instances — Oct 2025

Method B — Overwriting Late Entry

Genuine late entry (e.g., 09:34 or 10:20) replaced with fabricated earlier timestamp via admin access.

2 instances — Oct 2025

Method C — Cross-Day Back-Dating

Previous day's absence not recorded. Next day, both days' fabricated entries submitted together.

1 instance — Nov 2025

Step-by-Step

  1. Arrives late / absent — no morning scan
  2. Logs into ATLAS at end of day with admin credentials
  3. Enters fabricated "Time In" just before 09:00
  4. Report shows on-time — late/absence hidden from HR
  5. Full salary maintained; deductions avoided

Control Failures

  • No Segregation of Duties: IT custodian could modify own records
  • No Audit Alert: Back-dated entries triggered no flag
  • Unrestricted Admin Access: Records overwritten with no secondary authorization

Conclusion

Overall Assessment & Recommendations

Assessment

Further Investigation — Strongly Recommended

  • Preliminary findings indicate a sustained pattern of attendance record manipulation through misuse of administrative access rights.
  • A show-cause notice was issued to Mr. Shakeel. His submitted response reflected acceptance of the identified irregularities.
  • Any disciplinary action shall be taken strictly in accordance with the company's Code of Conduct and applicable HR policies.
  • A detailed forensic audit is recommended to substantiate findings, identify full extent of manipulation, evaluate possible involvement of other parties, and quantify complete financial impact.

What Remains Unclear

  • Full extent of manipulation across 17-month dataset
  • Possible involvement of other parties
  • Complete financial impact beyond the estimated Rs. 220,000
  • Integrity of all system audit logs (forensic review pending)
  • Authorization history for subject's admin access

Future Control & Governance Recommendations

  • Implement a complete approval workflow for attendance adjustments with documented justification and digital approval trail.
  • Strengthen approval hierarchy by restricting back-date access and enforcing multi-level authorization for attendance amendments.
  • Maintain detailed audit trails for all attendance edits — including user ID, date, time, and change history.
  • Introduce automated exception reports for unusual attendance patterns and repeated manual overrides.
  • Segregate system administration rights from attendance approval authority to minimize misuse of power.
  • Conduct periodic compliance reviews and independent audits of attendance system activities.
